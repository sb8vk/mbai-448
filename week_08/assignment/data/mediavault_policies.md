# MediaVault Business Policies

*Last updated: Q4 2025. Consult the VP of Operations for any policy clarifications.*

---

## Rental Policies

**Standard Rental Period:** 5 days from date of checkout. Rentals not returned within this window are considered overdue.

**Late Fees:** A flat fee of $1.00 per day is assessed for each day a rental is overdue, up to a maximum of the film's replacement cost. Late fees are applied automatically to the customer's account.

**Replacement Cost:** If a rental is not returned within 30 days of the due date, it is classified as lost. The customer's account is charged the full replacement cost of the film. Replacement costs vary by title and are recorded in the film catalog.

## Customer Policies

**VIP Customer Criteria:** A customer qualifies as VIP if they meet *any* of the following:
- Total lifetime spending exceeds $150
- Total lifetime rentals exceed 35
- Active account with at least one rental in each of the last 3 consecutive months

VIP customers should receive priority service and are eligible for promotional offers. Store managers should be able to identify VIP customers on request.

**At-Risk Customer Criteria:** A customer is considered at-risk of churn if they have:
- An active account (at least 5 lifetime rentals) AND
- No rental activity in the past 90 days

At-risk customers should be flagged for re-engagement outreach. Contact information for outreach purposes should only be released to the relevant store manager, not in bulk reports.

**Customer Data Handling:** Customer personally identifiable information (name, email, address, phone number) should not be included in aggregate reports or exported in bulk. Individual customer records may be accessed for specific operational needs (e.g., contacting a customer about an overdue rental) but must be justified by a legitimate business purpose.

## Inventory and Catalog Policies

**Inventory Utilization Target:** Each store should aim for an inventory utilization rate (percentage of inventory rented out at any given time) of at least 40%. Categories or titles with utilization consistently below 20% over a 90-day period should be flagged for review.

**Category Performance:** Category performance is evaluated on a combination of rental volume, revenue generated, and inventory utilization. No single metric is sufficient — a high-revenue category with low utilization may still warrant review, and a high-volume category with low revenue may indicate a pricing issue.

**New Title Acquisition:** Recommendations for acquiring new titles should be based on demonstrated demand patterns (high rental velocity in related categories, customer request data if available) rather than on assumed popularity.

---

## Store Performance

**Key Performance Indicators:** Store performance is evaluated across four dimensions:
1. **Revenue**: Total payment volume for the evaluation period
2. **Rental Volume**: Total number of transactions
3. **Customer Base**: Active customers (at least one rental in the evaluation period) and customer growth/churn
4. **Inventory Efficiency**: Utilization rate and overdue/lost inventory rate

**Performance Review Cadence:** Store performance reviews are conducted monthly. Comparisons between stores should normalize for store size (number of staff, inventory volume) where possible.

**Benchmarks:** No fixed numerical benchmarks are currently established. Performance is evaluated comparatively across stores and against the store's own historical trend. A store is considered underperforming if it shows a declining trend on two or more KPIs for two or more consecutive months.

---

## Staffing and Personnel

**Personnel Data:** Information about individual staff performance (rentals processed, hours worked, etc.) is considered sensitive and should not be surfaced in routine operational reports. Queries about individual staff performance should be escalated to the VP of Operations.

**Aggregate Staffing Metrics:** Store-level staffing metrics (total staff count, aggregate transactions per staff member) may be included in store performance reviews but should not identify individual employees.

---

## Communication Guidelines

**Tone**: Customer communications should be warm, professional, and clear. Avoid jargon, technical terms, or references to internal systems. Address the customer by name when possible.

**Structure**: Every customer response should include:
1. An acknowledgment of the customer's issue or question
2. A clear explanation of what was found or what action was taken
3. Concrete next steps (what the customer should expect, or what they need to do)
4. A sign-off with an invitation to follow up if needed

**Restrictions**:
- Never include internal investigation details, SQL queries, or system references in customer-facing responses
- Never reference other customers' accounts, transactions, or personal information
- Never make commitments the company cannot keep (e.g., promising a refund before it's approved, guaranteeing a timeline for resolution)
- Never share staff names or internal personnel information with customers
- If a resolution requires manager approval (e.g., refunds above $20, exceptions to policy), acknowledge the request and explain that it has been forwarded for review rather than promising the outcome

---

## Escalation Policies

The following types of requests should be escalated to a human analyst rather than answered directly:

- Any request involving modification of data (adding, changing, or deleting records)
- Bulk export of customer contact information
- Individual staff performance evaluations
- Financial projections or forward-looking estimates (the agent should present historical data but not make predictions)
- Any query where the agent is uncertain about the correct interpretation of a business policy
